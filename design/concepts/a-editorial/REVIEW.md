# Daftar Advisory — Live Website vs. Editorial Redesign

Assessment recorded 25 October 2026. Qualitative branding judgements, not measured
consumer response or audited performance. The live site at daftaradvisory.com was
checked on 25 October 2026; the editorial scores are based on the concept in this folder.

**Recommendation:** keep the current website's clarity and positioning discipline, but
adopt the editorial concept's stronger visual storytelling and service presentation.

The two have different strengths:

- Current website: more restrained, focused, commercially credible and easier to
  understand quickly.
- Editorial concept: more distinctive, visually sophisticated and effective at making
  Daftar's expertise tangible.

The editorial design is the stronger creative direction, but it should not replace the
current site unchanged.

The current site's headline:

> Finance work that feels simple, even when the issue is not.

It then identifies statements, technical review, audit readiness and quality of
earnings, followed by a clear boutique-practice promise. This communicates the business
with impressive economy.

The editorial alternative:

> Numbers your auditor, investor and tax authority accept.

This has greater immediate commercial tension, but also makes a stronger claim than
Daftar can necessarily control.

For positioning, the current headline. For creative expression, the editorial design.

### The biggest strategic difference: service positioning

This is more consequential than typography or page layout.

- **Current site: four focused services.** Financial statements, technical review, audit
  readiness and quality of earnings. Signals specialist expertise, selectivity and a
  tightly controlled practice.
- **Editorial design: eight client problems.** Reporting and audit, growth and profit,
  tax and compliance, plus retainer support. Signals broader commercial capability and
  more possible reasons to engage.

The four-service model is coherent and especially credible for a founder-led boutique.
The redesign risks stretching that clarity unless the broader services reflect actual
delivery capability. Do not broaden the offering to make the design look more
comprehensive; add lanes only when they represent a genuine strategic expansion.

## 3. Expected reception by audience

For senior decision-makers in Jordan, Saudi Arabia and the UAE:

- CFOs and financial controllers: the current site is easier to classify immediately.
  It communicates a focused technical practice.
- CEOs and founders: the editorial version has more potential to attract attention,
  because it translates technical finance work into practical business problems.
- Audit partners and technically sophisticated professionals: the editorial memo can
  demonstrate rigour, provided the analysis, references and conclusions are correct.
- Investors and transaction professionals: the editorial presentation looks more
  differentiated, but verified case evidence matters more than style.

These are expected reactions, not research findings.

## 4. What the new design should borrow from the live site

1. **Positioning discipline.** The promise of senior work, defined scope and a working
   file is consistent across the home page, About and the scope builder.
2. **Explicit professional boundaries.** The live site states clearly that Daftar is a
   non-attest practice and does not issue audit opinions. Preserve that, especially
   where audit-related services appear.
3. **The scope builder's simplicity.** It already structures a prospective engagement
   around type of work, complication and timing, with exclusions and indicative delivery
   periods. A useful commercial mechanism, not something that needs reinvention.

## 5. Recommended final direction: Daftar Editorial, refined

Editorial appearance. Specialist positioning. Evidence-led credibility. The more
distinctive design language of the concept, governed by the commercial clarity of the
existing practice.

The combination to approve:

- Editorial typography, technical memo and folded-file identity.
- The existing core four-service proposition, unless expanded scope is verified.
- The current brand promise, refined rather than replaced by an assurance-like claim.
- A small number of genuine, anonymised case examples.
- The existing three-step scope-building logic.
- Responsive mobile layouts, real form submission and a complete Arabic experience.

**Final judgement.** The editorial design has the higher brand-building ceiling. The
current live site has the stronger execution discipline and simpler commercial message.
The right next version is an editorial evolution of the existing website, not a
wholesale replacement. Approve the editorial concept as the new visual foundation, but
preserve the current site's service focus and positioning until there is a clear
commercial reason to change them.
